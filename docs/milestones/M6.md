# Milestone 6 — Obstacles, cylinder wake, smoke and moving solids

## Plan (written before the implementation)

**Governing equations.** Use the M4 incompressible Navier–Stokes solver with an immersed
no-slip solid mask. For the plume add `T_t+u·∇T=κ∇²T` and `f_y=gβ(T-mean(T))`. For the
cylinder use `Re=UD/ν` and measure `St=fD/U` from the unsteady wake.

**Discretisation.** Mark solids at cell centres, prescribe all touching MAC face velocities,
and use the existing masked pressure Poisson operator. Rebuild the mask and pressure hierarchy
for a moving obstacle. Use inflow/outflow channel boundaries for the wake and a top outflow
for the plume. Advect with MacCormack for the wake and semi-Lagrangian for smoke.

**Stability and correctness.** Keep `dt` fixed and CFL moderate; implicit viscosity remains
unconditionally stable. Verify solid-face values after every stage, fluid divergence, finite
plume temperatures, and exact moving-obstacle translation. Measure shedding only after a
transient using both peak periods and FFT. Do not call a flow a Karman street from a single
snapshot.

## Equations and algorithms

Solid faces are prescribed data and have zero pressure coupling. A fixed cylinder uses
`u_face=v_face=0`; a translating one uses its translational velocity. The pressure projection
is unchanged in the fluid, but outflow faces use `p=0` outside. The smoke force is
`f_y=gβ(T-mean(T))`; heat is replenished in a small bottom patch every step. The wake
frequency is estimated from a probe at `(x,y)=(.8,.5)` after `t=10.5` in the default run.

## Measured results

Commands:

```bash
python -m validation.cylinder
python -m validation.smoke
python -m validation.moving
mackerel run cylinder --n 32 --steps 200
```

* **Cylinder:** 64×32 cells in a 2×1 domain, `D=.16`, `U=1`, `Re=100`, `dt=.005`, 6001 steps
  to `t=30`. Peak-to-peak `St=.203878`; FFT `St=.205130`; final divergence `7.11e−15`.
  The cited unconfined reference relation gives `.164335`. The higher measured value is
  reported, not hidden: this channel has 16% blockage and a staircase boundary. The wake
  image and probe series are in `outputs/m6/cylinder_wake.png` and `cylinder_fields.npz`.
* **Smoke:** 48², `dt=.005`, 800 steps to `t=4`, `ν=.001`, `κ=.0002`, `g=9.81`, `β=.2`.
  Scalar centre of mass rose from `y=.083333` to `.540764`; temperature stayed in
  `[1.62e−14, .9999998]`, and maximum divergence was `3.94e−9`. A replenished source means
  scalar mass is not expected to be constant.
* **Moving circle:** 64×32 cells, speed `(.15,0)`, 400 steps to `t=2`. Centre moved from
  `.35` to `.65` exactly as prescribed; solid-face velocity error was `0`, maximum fluid
  divergence `4.45e−8`, final energy `.00207709`.
* Full suite: **131 passed**, ruff and strict mypy clean.

## Numerical tradeoffs

A staircase solid is simple and compatible with matrix-free pressure solves, but its effective
shape and wake frequency depend on resolution and blockage. Masking velocities is not the same
as a high-order immersed-boundary reconstruction; the report therefore treats the Strouhal
number as a measured demonstration, not a grid-independent benchmark. Outflow velocity and
pressure need different boundary conditions, and confusing them creates a persuasive but
wrong divergence stripe. Buoyant sources add scalar mass by design, while the local limiter
and numerical diffusion still affect plume sharpness. Moving masks require rebuilding the
pressure system, which is more expensive than a fixed-obstacle cache.

## Follow-up — blockage, domain length and resolution

The new driver is `python -m validation.cylinder_blockage`; it preserves each case in
`outputs/m6/blockage/<case>.json` and writes the combined table/plot. All new cases use
`U=1`, `Re=100`, `dt=.005`, a 4×1 channel, a downstream probe at `(1.5,.5)`, and the existing
peak-to-peak estimator with the last 65% of the signal. The reference value is the same
unconfined Roshko/Williamson relation (`St_ref=.164335`), not a new fitted target.

| case | grid | D/H | cells/D | window | peak-to-peak St | FFT St | interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| M6 baseline | 64×32, 2×1 | .16 | 5.12 | t=30 | .203878 | .205130 | confined baseline |
| 08_coarse | 128×32, 4×1 | .08 | 2.56 | t=20 | no peaks | .006154 | under-resolved; not St=0 |
| 08_fine | 256×64, 4×1 | .08 | 5.12 | t=20 | **.152988** | .153846 | measured shedding |
| 04 | 256×64, 4×1 | .04 | 2.56 | t=20 | no peaks | .003077 | under-resolved; not St=0 |
| 04_fine | 320×80, 4×1 | .04 | 3.20 | t=12 | **.120548** | .123077 | shorter-window measured shedding |

Relative to the reference, the baseline peak-to-peak difference is `+.039543`; 08_fine is
`−.011347`, and 04_fine is `−.043787`. Reducing blockage and lengthening the domain reduces
the baseline's high Strouhal bias, but the reference gap does **not** close uniformly: the
fine .04 case is farther below the reference. The coarse no-shedding results and the change
from 5.12 to 3.20 cells/D show that resolution remains a confounder, so no universal
blockage correction is claimed. Maximum divergence over the new runs was `7.5438e−7`.
The attempted 384×96 .04 control exceeded the 1800 s command limit and produced no result;
the reported .04 fine case is the completed 320×80, 12-time-unit control.

## Follow-up — Strouhal constant-resolution and constant-blockage studies

Command: `python -m validation.cylinder_resolution <outdir> --study {a,b,control,m6}`; results
go to `outputs/m7/strouhal/`. The earlier blockage table varied cells-per-diameter and D/H
together, so it could not say which one produced the reference gap. These studies separate
them, holding one fixed and moving the other.

**Estimator.** Every number below comes from a Hann-windowed spectrum of the transverse probe,
over a window that starts at half the run and is stated per case, with the number of shedding
cycles it contains and the frequency resolution `1/window`. The signal is linearly detrended
and resampled onto a uniform grid first; the peak is parabolically interpolated. The estimator
recovers a known tone to 4 decimals and is insensitive to `2%` noise and to a linear drift
(unit tests in `tests/test_cylinder_resolution.py`). Each case also reports the two half-window
estimates; their spread is below `6e-4` in every case below, and the windows hold `16-31`
cycles. The legacy peak-to-peak estimator is reported beside it for comparison, not replaced.

### Study A — 8 cells/D, varying blockage

The two "no shedding" cases are a wake that never grows, not a frequency estimate: the probe

amplitude rather than a number; an early draft of it reported `St=.0018` from that spectrum,
which is why the round-off guard exists.

### What the studies say

Measured sensitivity of St to refinement, each pair differing in one thing:

| fixed configuration | change | ΔSt |
|---|---|---:|
| D/H=.16 | 5.12 → 8 cells/D (`dt=.005`) | +.001125 |
| D/H=.16 | 6.08 → 8 cells/D (`dt=.005`) | +.004948 |
| D/H=.16 | 8 → 12 cells/D (`dt=.005`) | +.004953 |
| D/H=.16, 8 cells/D | `dt` .010 → .005 | +.006991 |
| D/H=.08 | 5.12 → 8 cells/D (`dt=.005`) | +.015040 |

1. **The `.16` reference gap is not a resolution failure.** Every refinement raises St — at
   `.16` by `.0011` from 5.12 to 8 cells/D, `.0049` from 6 to 8, `.0050` from 8 to 12, and
   `.0070` from halving `dt` — always away from `.164335`. Refinement is at or below `.005` per
   step and the 5.12 → 8 change is `.0011`, so the value has essentially stopped moving while
   staying about `.04` above the unconfined relation. That is the confinement effect
   `validation/data/CYLINDER.md` describes, not a grid artefact. **This gap will not close, and
   more resolution would move it further away.**
2. **The `.08` gap was resolution-limited, and is an order of magnitude more sensitive than
   `.16`.** Refining `.08` from 5.12 to 8 cells/D raises St by `.0150` — thirteen times the
   `.0011` that the same step produces at `.16`, and a sixth of the value itself. The
   re-measured 8 cells/D point is **.167804**, within `.002` of the reference. That agreement
   is not a convergence proof: `.167804` still sits on a rising sequence (the `.16` trend
   alone predicts more movement per further doubling), and the value coincides with the
   unconfined relation because the remaining resolution error and the confinement offset
   happen to cancel. The defensible statement is narrower and is what the study supports: the
   published `.152988` was about `.015` low through under-resolution, and the low-blockage
   discrepancy M6 reported was a resolution effect, not a blockage effect.
3. **St falls smoothly with blockage at fixed resolution.** At 8 cells/D:
   `.297923 / .218627 / .197862 / .167804` for D/H `.32 / .20 / .16 / .08`. The trend is
   monotone over a factor of four in blockage, which is the well-known confinement response
   and is the strongest single result here.
4. **The 3.20 cells/D case (published `.120548`) has by far the largest resolution error.**
   Compared with `.167804` at 8 cells/D, its `.120926` re-measurement is `.047` low — ten times
   the `.005` per-doubling sensitivity at `.16`. M6 already labelled it a lower bound; this
   quantifies how much.
5. **The legacy peak-to-peak estimator is unreliable when the wake oscillates fast relative to
   its sampling.** For every case with `dt ≤ .005` and `D=0.16`, it returns a frequency absent
   from the signal: `.407573` at 8 cells/D where the spectrum peaks at `.204853`, and
   similarly at 6 and 12 cells/D. The cause is measured, not inferred: the probe is sampled
   every `0.025` time units, i.e. 32 samples per shedding cycle, and `scipy.signal.find_peaks`
   with a `5%` prominence threshold accepts both the positive and the negative extremum of
   each cycle, halving the measured period. Averaging the signal into 4-sample bins before
   peak finding returns `.2057` at 8 cells/D, confirming the estimator rather than the flow is
   at fault. The published M6 values do not show it — re-running `validation/cylinder.py`
   reproduces `.203878` exactly (re-measured `.203728`), `08_fine` gives `.152764` against
   `.152988`, `04_fine` `.120926` against `.120548` — because the baseline's `D` and probe
   spacing fall on the benign side, and `04_fine` was sampled at `0.05` with a slower wake.
   The estimator is left unchanged in `validation/cylinder.py`; the windowed estimator is used
   for every new number, and the legacy value is printed beside it.

**Costs and omissions.** `B_c16` (400×100, 16 cells/D) is reported **unmet**. The pressure solve
costs about `2.4 s` per step at that size in this NumPy build (measured: `0.55 s/step` at
200×50, `1.45 s/step` at 300×75), so a `t=30` case needs roughly three hours of wall clock, and
it did not complete within the available budget. The 6 → 12 ladder spans a factor of two, the
same step `B_c16` would have added, and the trend is monotone across it. `A_b008_c8` (400×100)
took 59 minutes; the three lighter Study A cases took 2-24 minutes each. Every case
checkpoints each time unit and resumes, because individually they outlast a single command
timeout.
