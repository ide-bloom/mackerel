# Milestone 1 — Foundations and the grid

**Status:** done · **Commit:** see `git log --oneline -- docs/milestones/M1.md`

## Plan (written before coding)

* **Governing equations.** Nothing is time-stepped yet. The milestone establishes the
  discrete stage on which Navier–Stokes will be solved: the incompressibility constraint
  `∇·u = 0`, the discrete divergence and gradient operators, and the exact reference fields
  (Taylor–Green, solid-body rotation, corner-curl fields) that every later milestone tests
  against.
* **Discretisation.** MAC staggered grid on square cells with `h = lx/nx`: `u` on vertical
  faces `(ny, nx+1)`, `v` on horizontal faces `(ny+1, nx)`, scalars at centres `(ny, nx)`,
  vorticity at corners `(ny+1, nx+1)`. Second-order centred differences throughout, midpoint
  rule for all integrals.
* **Stability conditions.** None apply yet — no time stepping. The relevant *accuracy*
  conditions are: `h` small enough to resolve the mode of interest (≥ 8 cells per
  wavelength), and ghost-cell interpolation used consistently for anything sampled off-grid.
* **Correctness tests.** Exactness of bilinear interpolation on `a + bx + cy + dxy`;
  second-order interpolation error on a smooth field; machine-precision divergence of
  corner-curl fields; `O(h²)` divergence residual of a continuum div-free field; a per-kind
  boundary-condition test suite; a numerical demonstration that the staggered gradient has no
  checkerboard null space; second-order convergence of Taylor–Green energy, enstrophy and
  velocity error.

## (b) The equation or algorithm each piece implements

**Staggered layout.** `u[j,i]` at `(i h, (j+½)h)`; `v[j,i]` at `((i+½)h, j h)`; scalars at
`((i+½)h, (j+½)h)`; `ω` at `(i h, j h)`.

**Discrete divergence** (cells):
`div[j,i] = (u[j,i+1] − u[j,i])/h + (v[j+1,i] − v[j,i])/h`

**Discrete gradient** (faces): `(∇p)_x[j,i] = (p[j,i] − p[j,i−1])/h`, `(∇p)_y[j,i] = (p[j,i] − p[j−1,i])/h`.

**Bilinear interpolation** on a cell of the sampled array with fractional indices
`(fx, fy)`: `w00 = (1−tx)(1−ty)`, `w10 = tx(1−ty)`, `w01 = (1−tx)ty`, `w11 = tx·ty`.

**Corner-curl velocity** (exactly divergence free):
`u[j,i] = (ψ[j+1,i] − ψ[j,i])/h`, `v[j,i] = −(ψ[j,i+1] − ψ[j,i])/h`.

**Vorticity** at corners: `ω = (v[j,i] − v[j,i−1])/h − (u[j,i] − u[j−1,i])/h`.

**Boundary conditions.** Normal velocity on a wall face set directly to the wall's normal
speed; tangential and scalar conditions through ghost values
`ghost = 2·wall − interior` (no-slip/inflow), `ghost = interior` (free-slip, outflow),
wrap (periodic).

**Diagnostics.** `E = Σ ½(u_c² + v_c²) h²`, `Z = Σ ½ω² h²`, `C = dt·max(|u|+|v|)/h`,
`∫φ = Σ φ h²`.

**Analytic reference.** Taylor–Green: `ψ = (A/k) sin kx sin ky`,
`u = A sin kx cos ky`, `v = −A cos kx sin ky`, `E(t) = (A²/4) e^{−4k²νt}`,
`Z(t) = 2k²E(t)`.

## (c) Three to six sentences on the numerical tradeoffs

Staggering costs bookkeeping — three different array shapes, half-cell offsets in every
interpolation — and buys the elimination of the checkerboard null space, which is a
structural defect rather than a small error, so the trade is not close. Bilinear
interpolation is only second-order accurate and mildly diffusive, which is exactly the
property that later makes semi-Lagrangian advection stable but blurry; the alternative
(higher-order interpolation) buys accuracy at the cost of overshoot near sharp features,
which is why Milestone 2 pairs MacCormack with a limiter instead of simply raising the
stencil order. Sampling exact fields from a *corner* streamfunction rather than evaluating
the analytic formulas on the faces makes test fields exactly divergence-free but introduces
an `O(h²)` bias in the sampled velocity (measured: 1.7e-2 → 7.1e-5 as h goes 1/8 → 1/128,
order 1.98); I accepted the bias because exact incompressibility is the property the pressure
solver must be tested against, and the bias is demonstrably second order. Building ghosts by
mirroring about the wall value rather than copying the wall sample is a half-cell question
that changes nothing at a straight wall but decides whether a moving lid is felt at the right
speed. Finally, computing enstrophy on interior corners only is a deliberate choice to avoid
inventing wall vorticity, which means reported enstrophy on wall-bounded flows is slightly
lower than the continuum value — acceptable because enstrophy is used comparatively
(decay, amplification) and never as an absolute benchmark.

## Assumptions / deviations recorded in this milestone

* **D1** face-indexed staggered arrays (`u[:,i]` is the face at `x = i h`).
* **D2** exact div-free test fields come from a corner streamfunction; the "Tay–Green
  residual" test was replaced by a Gaussian vortex because TG's residual was round-off, not
  error (a test that passes for the wrong reason).
* **D3** ghost cells mirror about the wall *value* with the partner one cell inside;
  periodic ghosts wrap by `nx`/`ny` (the true period), not `nx+1`.
* **D4** vorticity edges: periodic wrap where available, one-sided zero otherwise; enstrophy
  and vorticity images use interior corners.
* **D5** `ly` is derived from `h·ny`, not a constructor argument.
* **D6** `pad_scalar` must be sampled with `x0 = y0 = −h/2`.
* **D7** `ffmpeg` is absent in this environment → MP4 export is a no-op returning `None`;
  GIF only. Documented, not faked.
* **D8** error bounds in tests are `c·h²` with `c` from the measured error, plus a separate
  convergence-rate assertion.

## Questions you should be able to answer

1. Why does the checkerboard pressure mode have *zero* gradient on a collocated grid and
   the maximum possible gradient on a staggered grid, and what does that imply for the
   singularity of the pressure Poisson problem?
2. Why is the divergence of the corner-curl test field exactly zero while the divergence of
   the *analytically* divergence-free Taylor–Green field, sampled on the faces, is only
   `O(h²)`? (Hint: which construction makes the four terms cancel pairwise?)
3. `pad_velocity` mirrors ghosts as `2·wall − interior` where the partner is one cell inside
   the wall. What goes wrong at a moving lid if the partner is instead taken to be the wall
   sample itself, and why is the half-cell question invisible for a stationary wall?
4. Energy, enstrophy and interpolation error all converge at ≈2; what would a measured order
   of 1.0 have told you about one of the operators?
