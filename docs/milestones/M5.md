# Milestone 5 — Lid-driven cavity and Ghia et al. (1982)

## Plan (written before the implementation)

**Governing equations.** Steady incompressible Navier–Stokes in a unit square, with unit lid
speed and `ν=1/Re`; no-slip on all walls, with `u_top=1`.

**Discretisation.** Reuse the M4 MAC split solver with MacCormack velocity advection,
implicit viscosity and pressure projection. Average MAC velocities to cell centres and
interpolate the two centreline profiles to the published Ghia stations. Store the reference
CSV and its citation in the repository.

**Stability and stopping.** Use fixed `dt=.01`, a CFL-safe semi-Lagrangian trajectory,
unconditionally stable diffusion, and pressure tolerance `1e-8` with a measured iteration
budget. March until the maximum velocity change over 100 steps is below `2e-8`, with a
60-time-unit safety limit. Do not infer steady state from a single plot.

**Correctness tests.** Check reference-table completeness and orientation, exact wall
endpoints, finite/divergence-free short runs, and max/RMS centreline deviations at Re=100 and
400. Preserve the known Re=400 reference outlier in an all-point metric and show the primary
metric separately. Run the full tests, strict mypy and ruff.

## What was built

* `validation/lid_cavity.py`: reproducible cavity driver, steady-state monitor, Ghia

* `validation/data/ghia_1982.csv`: 102 cited reference values for Re=100, 400 and 1000.
* `validation/data/GHIA_1982.md`: bibliographic citation, source transcriptions and the

* `tests/test_cavity.py`: reference integrity, wall endpoints and short-run checks.

## Equations and algorithm

The physical parameters are `Re=U_lid L/ν`, `U_lid=L=1`, hence `ν=1/Re`. The top boundary is
`u=1,v=0`, while the other three are `u=v=0`. The numerical algorithm is exactly the M4
advect–force–diffuse–project step; there is no separate steady solver or reference-specific
correction. The comparison values are `u(x=.5,y)` and `v(x,y=.5)`, normalised by the lid speed.

## Measured results

Command: `python -m validation.lid_cavity` (default `n=48`; set `MACKEREL_CAVITY_N=64` for
the finer run). The long runs write `outputs/m5/cavity_ghia_profiles.png`,
`cavity_velocity.png`, and raw result files.

| Re | grid | time | max u error | RMS u error | max v error* | RMS v error* | max divergence |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 48² | 30.0 | 0.0035 | 0.0018 | 0.0086 | 0.0042 | 1.48e−7 |
| 400 | 48² | 61.0 | 0.0371 | 0.0226 | 0.0542 | 0.0317 | 1.05e−7 |
| 400 | 64² | 61.0 | 0.02897 | 0.01616 | 0.03820 | 0.02202 | 1.81e−7 |

`*` The primary v metric excludes only the cited printed outlier at `x=.9063`. At 64² Re=400,
including it gives max/RMS `0.10302/0.03287`; the CSV and all-point metrics retain it. At
48² the all-point max was `0.0868`. The finer run therefore improves both the physical
profile error and the visibility of the reference anomaly; no reference value was edited.

A separate exploratory 128² Re=400 run with the old 400-iteration cap gave u max/RMS
`0.02193/0.01007` and v max/RMS `0.02146/0.01213` after excluding the same point, but its
maximum divergence was `6.63e−5` because CG hit that cap. It is not used as the final passing
number; D22 records the cap correction. The full suite after this milestone is **121 passed**,
with strict mypy and ruff clean.

## Numerical tradeoffs

The cavity has no closed-form velocity field, so the benchmark is a comparison with a
published finite-grid reference rather than an exact-error proof. Coarse semi-Lagrangian
advection is too diffusive at Re=400; MacCormack reduces that error but costs additional
sweeps and still has interpolation/limiter error. Smaller time steps and finer grids improve
the profiles at substantial pressure-solve cost. The pressure tolerance and the steady-state
monitor measure different things: a small divergence does not prove that the flow has stopped
evolving, and a steady but under-resolved flow need not match Ghia. Reporting both centreline
components and the anomalous reference point prevents a single scalar score from hiding these
issues.

## Questions for the reader

1. Why is `ν=1/Re` sufficient to set the cavity Reynolds number in this nondimensionalisation?
2. How are MAC face velocities converted into the two Ghia centreline profiles without
   introducing a half-cell shift?
3. Why can a run be divergence-free but still not yet steady?
4. What is the difference between the primary and all-point Re=400 v error, and why is the
   exception documented rather than silently correcting the CSV?
5. Why does MacCormack improve the high-Re cavity result relative to semi-Lagrangian
   advection, and what additional error does its limiter introduce?
