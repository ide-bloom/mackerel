# Milestone 5 — Lid-driven cavity and Ghia et al. (1982)

## Plan (written before the implementation)

**Governing equations.** Steady incompressible Navier–Stokes in a unit square, with unit lid
speed and `ν=1/Re`; no-slip on all walls, with `u_top=1`.

**Discretisation.** Reuse the M4 MAC split solver with MacCormack velocity advection,
implicit viscosity and pressure projection. Average MAC velocities to cell centres and
interpolate the two centreline profiles to the published Ghia stations. Store the reference
CSV and its citation in the repository.

**Stability and stopping.** Use fixed `dt=.01`, a CFL-safe semi-Lagrangian trajectory,
unconditionally stable diffusion, and pressure tolerance `1e-8` with a measured iteration
budget. March until the maximum velocity change over 100 steps is below `2e-8`, with a
60-time-unit safety limit. Do not infer steady state from a single plot.

**Correctness tests.** Check reference-table completeness and orientation, exact wall
endpoints, finite/divergence-free short runs, and max/RMS centreline deviations at Re=100 and
400. Preserve the known Re=400 reference outlier in an all-point metric and show the primary
metric separately. Run the full tests, strict mypy and ruff.

## What was built

* `validation/lid_cavity.py`: reproducible cavity driver, steady-state monitor, Ghia
  interpolation, error tables, velocity plots and raw JSON/CSV/NPZ output.
* `validation/data/ghia_1982.csv`: 102 cited reference values for Re=100, 400 and 1000.
* `validation/data/GHIA_1982.md`: bibliographic citation, source transcriptions and the
  documented `Re=400, x=.9063` data-quality exception.
* `tests/test_cavity.py`: reference integrity, wall endpoints and short-run checks.

## Equations and algorithm

The physical parameters are `Re=U_lid L/ν`, `U_lid=L=1`, hence `ν=1/Re`. The top boundary is
`u=1,v=0`, while the other three are `u=v=0`. The numerical algorithm is exactly the M4
advect–force–diffuse–project step; there is no separate steady solver or reference-specific
correction. The comparison values are `u(x=.5,y)` and `v(x,y=.5)`, normalised by the lid speed.

Command: `python -m validation.lid_cavity` (default `n=48`; set `MACKEREL_CAVITY_N=64` for
the finer run). The long runs write `outputs/m5/cavity_ghia_profiles.png`,
`cavity_velocity.png`, and raw result files.

| 100 | 48² | 30.0 | 0.0035 | 0.0018 | 0.0086 | 0.0042 | 1.48e−7 |

`*` The primary v metric excludes only the cited printed outlier at `x=.9063`. At 64² Re=400,

A separate exploratory 128² Re=400 run with the old 400-iteration cap gave u max/RMS

maximum divergence was `6.63e−5` because CG hit that cap. It is not used as the final passing
number; D22 records the cap correction. The full suite after this milestone is **121 passed**,
with strict mypy and ruff clean.

## Numerical tradeoffs

The cavity has no closed-form velocity field, so the benchmark is a comparison with a
published finite-grid reference rather than an exact-error proof. Coarse semi-Lagrangian
advection is too diffusive at Re=400; MacCormack reduces that error but costs additional
sweeps and still has interpolation/limiter error. Smaller time steps and finer grids improve
the profiles at substantial pressure-solve cost. The pressure tolerance and the steady-state
monitor measure different things: a small divergence does not prove that the flow has stopped
evolving, and a steady but under-resolved flow need not match Ghia. Reporting both centreline
components and the anomalous reference point prevents a single scalar score from hiding these
issues.

## Follow-up — is the Ghia Re=400 `v(.9063)` datum an outlier, or is the solver wrong there?

Command: `python -m validation.ghia_outlier`; results in `outputs/m7/ghia/`. The milestone text
above excludes `v(x=.9063) = -.23827` from the primary error metric. That exclusion is only
justified if the *reference* is the problem, so this study tests both possibilities with one
yardstick: the secant slope of the `v` profile immediately upstream and downstream of the
station, which must be comparable on either side of a point that lies on a smooth curve.

**The published column has a kink that no other Reynolds number has.** Applying the same
measure to the three transcribed columns:

| Re | secant upstream | secant downstream | ratio | peak $|v|$ station |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | `+1.179` | `+1.693` | `.697` | .8047 |
| **400** | **`+4.513`** | **`+0.251`** | **`17.96`** | .8594 |
| 1000 | `-1.884` | `+3.157` | `.597` | .9063 |

A ratio of one means the two secants agree. In magnitude, Re=100 and Re=1000 sit at `.697` and
`.597` — the difference is curvature only — while Re=400 sits at `17.96`: the profile arrives
at the station **eighteen times steeper** than it leaves. Note also the sign: at Re=1000 the
secants have opposite signs because the peak of `|v|` *is* that station, so a sign flip alone is
not evidence of anything. What singles out Re=400 is the size of the ratio, not its sign. Its
`|v|` peak is at `.8594`, one station upstream, so `.9063` is on the flank of that peak, where
an eighteen-fold slope change cannot occur in a smooth solution.

**An independent interpolation of the same table, built without the datum, gives `-.365673`**
(monotone cubic through the remaining Re=400 stations). This is a property of the published
numbers alone; it involves no solver output.

**The computed profile converges on that interpolation, not on the printed value.** Grid ladder
at Re=400, same solver settings as the milestone runs, `t` to steady state (`1.7e−8` change):

| grid | `v(.9063)` | error vs printed `-.23827` | error vs `.365673` | max `|v|` error (primary) | converged |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 48² | `-.325069` | `-.086799` | `+.040605` | `.054172` | yes |
| 64² | `-.341290` | `-.103020` | `+.024384` | `.038197` | yes |
| 96² | `-.355656` | `-.117386` | `+.010017` | `.025301` | yes |
| 128² | `-.361466` | `-.123196` | **`+.004208`** | `.021456` | yes |

The computed value moves away from the printed datum with every refinement and towards the
reinterpolated one, reducing that error by a factor of about `10` from 48² to 128². Its own
kink ratio stays finite and small (`|ratio| .38-.42`, i.e. two secants of the same size and
sign), unlike the reference column's `17.96`, so the computed profile has no local feature at
`.9063` either. The levels are first-order at best (`.0406 → .0244 → .0100 → .0042`, ratio
about `2.4` per doubling); a finer ladder would be needed to call this asymptotically
converged, and that is not claimed. Maximum divergence over the ladder was `5.44e−7` and no
pressure cap was hit.

**Status: the datum is an outlier; the solver is not wrong there.** The primary-metric exclusion
is justified by evidence internal to the published table and confirmed by convergence, not by a
preference for our own output. `validation/data/ghia_1982.csv` is unchanged, still carries the
printed value, and the all-point metrics still include it (`v_max_error_all` grows from
`.0868` at 48² to `.1232` at 128² exactly because the printed point does not move while the
computed profile does — which is the expected signature of a bad datum, not of a converging
solver).
