# Milestone 7 — PIC/FLIP free-surface water

## Plan and acceptance criteria

Implement a particle-in-cell / fluid-implicit-particle liquid on the existing staggered MAC
grid, with a genuine atmospheric free-surface pressure condition. Validate an instantaneous
2-D dam break against the Martin–Moyce (1952) front series, provide splash and sloshing demos,
and measure an optional Numba transfer kernel against the pure-NumPy reference. Save raw time
series and particle data as well as figures/GIFs, and report errors rather than fitting the
simulation to the reference.

The acceptance checks are: finite particles and velocities, no particle loss in closed demos,
small liquid divergence after projection, a measured dam-break error table, reproducible demo
artifacts, NumPy/Numba equivalence checks, and a clean full test/lint/type pass.

## What was built

* `src/mackerel/flip.py`: structure-of-arrays particles, deterministic jittered seeding,
  bilinear MAC-face transfer, PIC/FLIP velocity updates, gravity, CFL-limited adaptive steps,
  closed-wall particle collisions, and nominal particle-volume diagnostics.
* `src/mackerel/project.py`: `free_surface=True` pressure systems. Liquid–air faces use an
  exterior `p=0` Dirichlet contribution; this is deliberately different from the zero-normal
  pressure gradient at an immersed solid. Only faces touching liquid are pressure-corrected.
* `src/mackerel/flip_numba.py`: optional, lazily compiled Numba transfer kernel. The default
  backend remains pure NumPy and Numba is not a base dependency.
* `validation/flip.py`: dam-break PIC and FLIP drivers, splash and tilted-water sloshing
  scenes, raw CSV/NPZ output, PNG figures and GIF animations.
* `validation/performance.py`: warmed-up NumPy/Numba transfer and complete-step benchmarks,
  including equality checks and separate JIT compilation timing.
* `validation/data/martin_moyce_1952.csv`: 14 `(tau,delta)` reference points with citation,
  digitisation source, normalization and gate-delay caveat.
* `mackerel run dam_break`, `mackerel run splash`, and `mackerel run sloshing` CLI scenes.
* `tests/test_flip.py`: interpolation, atmospheric-pressure, volume, short-run, final-time,
  and optional Numba regressions.

## Equations and algorithm

For each particle `p`, bilinear weights `w_{p,f}` transfer velocity to a MAC face `f`:

`u_f = sum_p(w_{p,f} u_p) / sum_p(w_{p,f})`.

The grid velocity is saved, gravity is added explicitly (`v <- v - g dt` on weighted `v`
faces), and the liquid pressure projection is solved. If `alpha` is the PIC fraction, the
particle update is

`u_p_new = alpha*u_grid_new + (1-alpha)*(u_p_old + u_grid_new - u_grid_old)`.

Thus `alpha=1` is pure PIC and `alpha=.05` is the reported 95%-FLIP blend. Particles are then
advected with their updated velocities. The default adaptive step is
`min(dt_max, .5*h/max|u_particle|)`; an explicitly supplied `dt` is respected and the final
step is clamped by the drivers to the requested end time.

In a liquid cell, `-laplacian(p) = -div(u*)/dt`. A liquid–air face contributes a diagonal
Dirichlet term with pressure zero on the air side. Air cells have no physical pressure
unknown and are returned as exactly `p=0`; air–air faces do not receive a pressure correction.
Domain walls still impose their normal velocity condition. Interpolation indices and their
fractional weights are clamped together at domain edges, preventing negative weights for
particles within half a cell of a wall.

## Reproduction

From the repository root:

```bash
python -m validation.flip                 # dam break, splash, sloshing -> outputs/m7
python -m validation.performance          # optional Numba benchmark -> outputs/m7
mackerel run dam_break --n 48 --steps 200
mackerel run splash --n 40 --steps 200
mackerel run sloshing --n 40 --steps 200
python -m pytest -q
ruff check .
mypy
```

The full validation driver runs both methods on a 48x36 grid, with a `1 x .75` domain,
initial column `a=.25` wide and `2a=.5` high, `g=9.81`, `dt_max=.0005`, and 669 steps to
`tau=2.96`. The front diagnostic is the rightmost particle plus `h/2`, divided by `a`;
`tau=t*sqrt(2g/a)`. No gate-opening delay is added.

## Measured dam-break results

The reference is the documented 14-point Martin–Moyce digitisation. Errors are computed by
interpolating each simulation's complete time series to the reference `tau` values. The
initial half-cell front convention gives `delta=1.024931` at `tau=0`; this discretisation
offset is included in the metrics rather than removed.

| quantity | 95% FLIP (`alpha=.05`) | pure PIC (`alpha=1`) |
|---|---:|---:|
| reference points | 14 | 14 |
| front RMSE | **0.072551** | **0.622552** |
| maximum absolute front error | **0.113702** | **1.144789** |
| mean relative front error, excluding `tau=0` | **3.3447%** | **19.5755%** |
| maximum liquid divergence during run | `3.3662e-7` | `9.0968e-8` |
| final liquid divergence | `2.9167e-8` | `7.3712e-9` |
| nominal volume relative error | `0` | `0` |
| wall time in this sandbox | `3.246 s` | `3.208 s` |

FLIP's largest positive deviation is `+0.113702` at `tau=2.20` (predicted `2.673702`,
reference `2.56`). PIC increasingly lags the experimental front, ending at `2.525211` versus
`3.67` at `tau=2.96`. These are measured outcomes of the two updates, not a claim that one
universally converges to the experiment. The reference is a secondary digitisation, and the
particle-front definition is not identical to a laboratory interface threshold.

## NumPy versus Numba

Numba `0.68.1` and NumPy `2.4.6` were used on the sandbox's Linux x86-64 environment. JIT
compilation took `.19285 s` and was excluded from steady-state timings. Transfer timings are
medians of seven runs of the `u`-face transfer; both `u` and `v` outputs were checked first.

| grid | particles | NumPy (ms) | Numba (ms) | speedup | max checked difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32x32 | 4,096 | `.452831` | `.036094` | `12.55x` | `2.66e-15` |
| 64x64 | 16,384 | `2.008120` | `.161267` | `12.45x` | `1.78e-15` |
| 128x128 | 65,536 | `10.200012` | `.736513` | `13.85x` | `2.66e-15` |

A complete 32x24 water run of 40 steps (512 particles, including the same pressure solve)
took `.096852 s` with NumPy and `.090994 s` with Numba: only `1.064x` faster. The largest
particle-component discrepancy was `3.053e-16`. The transfer kernel is therefore substantially
faster in isolation, but pressure projection dominates this small full solve; the transfer
speedup must not be advertised as a whole-solver speedup. Without the optional dependency,
the benchmark reports that no accelerated timing is available and the NumPy solver still works.

## Numerical limitations and tradeoffs

* This is a 2-D, inviscid particle liquid with no surface tension, contact-angle model, air
  momentum, turbulence model, or body-fitted free surface. Particle occupancy gives a
  cell-scale interface and can produce FLIP noise or PIC numerical diffusion.
* No conservative volume-of-fluid remapping is performed. Closed-wall collisions clamp
  positions and zero the outward normal component, so exact particle-count / nominal-volume
  conservation is a property of the representation, not an independent proof of conservative
  interface transport.
* Particle reseeding exists as an explicit **opt-in** paired exchange (see the paired-reseeding section below
  and `docs/DESIGN.md` D37). It is off by default so every figure above is reproducible, and
  when enabled it moves particles without creating or deleting any. Its measured effect at
  48×36/96×72 is small and not uniformly favourable, so it is not enabled in the milestone
  configuration.
* Divergence is measured on liquid cells after each pressure solve; it is not zero to machine
  precision because the relative CG tolerance is finite and the free-surface mask changes as
  particles move. The maximum values above are retained in the raw CSV files.
* The dam-break comparison is a single resolution and a single front convention, not a
  grid-convergence or experimental-uncertainty study. The half-cell offset, staircase
  occupancy, lack of viscosity, and secondary digitisation all affect the deviation.
* Numba accelerates only particle transfer. It is lazily imported, serial (to avoid racing
  shared-face accumulations), and checked against NumPy before timing.

## Questions for the reader

1. Why does a liquid–air face add a pressure diagonal term while a liquid–solid face removes
   its coupling?
2. What is the difference between the PIC and FLIP particle velocity updates, and why does
   pure PIC lag the dam-break front here?
3. Why must interpolation fractions be clamped along with indices at a wall?
4. Why is exact particle-count conservation weaker than conservative free-surface volume
   transport?
5. Why can a 13x transfer speedup produce only a 1.06x complete-step speedup?

## Follow-up — second dam-break resolution and gate-time convention

Command: `python -m validation.dam_break_resolution`. The same instantaneous-release
protocol was run at 48×36 and 96×72 for both methods, with `a=.25`, initial height `2a`,
`g=9.81`, `dt_max=.0005`, `end_tau=2.96`, and 95% FLIP (`pic_blend=.05`) or PIC (`1.0`).

**Gate convention.** `t=0` is the initial instantaneous-release configuration. We use
`tau=(t+0)*sqrt(2g/a)` and `delta=(x_rightmost_particle+h/2)/a`; the experimental reference
is used as published. Lethe's optional `+.175` gate-opening correction is **not** added to
the simulated time and is not added to the reference CSV. Thus the comparison has zero gate
delay by construction, rather than a fitted time shift.

| method | grid | steps | front RMSE | max absolute error | mean relative error | volume relative error | max divergence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FLIP | 48×36 | 669 | .0725510 | .1137017 | .0334468 | 0 | 3.3662e−7 |
| FLIP | 96×72 | 669 | .2564548 | .3385197 | .1084325 | 0 | 3.2514e−6 |
| PIC | 48×36 | 669 | .6225517 | 1.1447889 | .1957547 | 0 | 9.0968e−8 |
| PIC | 96×72 | 669 | .0868497 | .1852352 | .0282101 | 0 | 6.6776e−7 |

The refinement is **not uniformly convergent**: FLIP's front RMSE increases from `.0725510`
to `.2564548`, while PIC decreases from `.6225517` to `.0868497`. Both resolutions conserve
the nominal particle-volume measure exactly in this driver, but the finer FLIP run has a
larger maximum divergence. These are measured failures/limitations, not hidden by choosing
the better method or applying a gate delay. Raw per-case series, particles, reference
interpolations and the comparison plot are in `outputs/m7/resolution/`.

## Follow-up — conservative paired reseeding

Command: `python -m validation.flip_reseed`. Reseeding is **opt-in** (`reseed=False` by
default), so every result above is unchanged and reproducible; this section is the only place
the feature is switched on. Configuration (`validation/flip_reseed.py:CONFIG`):
`reseed_interval=1`, `min_particles_per_cell=2`, `max_particles_per_cell=8`, `reseed_target=4`
(the nominal `particles_per_cell`), `reseed_interior_only=True`, `reseed_radius=5` cells,
`reseed_cooldown=10` steps, `reseed_seed=0`.

**Conservation.** A reseeding step moves particles and never creates or deletes one. Particle
count, the particle-velocity multiset and the nominal volume are therefore unchanged by
construction, and each of the eight runs below reports `volume_relative_error = 0`.

**Deterministic screen.** A hand-built 12×9 slab with a planted interior hole, a starved
interior cell and one overfull donor cell moves exactly 3 particles, keeps 225 particles and
a volume of `.390625`, fills both defects, and changes no fringe cell. Cell-density RMS around the nominal four falls
`1.1521 → .7676` and the coefficient of variation `.281 → .191`. The largest single move is `2.303h` (inside the `5h` radius).

**Dam break** (`a=.25`, height `2a`, `g=9.81`, `dt_max=.0005`, 95% FLIP, 669 steps,
instantaneous release, front = rightmost particle `+ h/2`):

| method | grid | front RMSE | max abs error | mean relative error | interior holes | starved interior | starved fringe | particles moved |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| control | 48×36 | .0725510 | .1137017 | .0334468 | 1 | 4 | 9 | 0 |
| reseeded | 48×36 | **.0690662** | **.1063975** | **.0320170** | 0 | 0 | 5 | 103 (9.0%) |
| control | 96×72 | .2564548 | .3385197 | .1084325 | 6 | 21 | 17 | 0 |
| reseeded | 96×72 | .2570376 | .3420841 | .1084463 | 0 | 0 | 15 | 521 (45.2%) |

Reseeding removes **every** interior hole and starved interior cell at both resolutions
(`7 → 0` defects at 48×36, `27 → 0` at 96×72) and lowers the maximum divergence from
`3.366e−7` to `6.458e−8` at 48×36 (unchanged order at 96×72). It improves the front at 48×36
and is *marginally worse* at 96×72 (`+0.227%` RMSE, `+0.013%` mean relative error). The 96×72 refinement failure documented
above is therefore **not** repaired by reseeding: this is a real result, not a hidden one.
Mean move distance is `.77h` (48×36) and `.76h` (96×72), maximum `3.95h`/`3.16h`, so the
exchange stays local.

**Splash** (20×15 closed box, falling drop, `t=.4`, 800 steps, paired with the identical
unreseeded run): starved interior cells `3 → 0`, liquid cells `94 → 97`, maximum divergence
unchanged at `2.841e−7`, peak speed unchanged at `1.89218`, particle-speed RMS `0.17054 →
0.16261`. 9 particles move, mean move `1.61h`. The interface rasters agree for the first 14
of 21 saved frames; where they differ the classified liquid/air masks disagree on at most
`1.67%` of pixels (mean over differing frames `0.86%`).

**Sloshing** (same box, `t=.4`): documented **null result**. The tilted initial condition
develops no interior defect at all, so only one particle moves in 800 steps and all 21
interface rasters are byte-identical. Reseeding is inert on this scene, which is the expected
behaviour of a defect-driven rule and is reported rather than presented as a benefit.

**Costs.** Reseeding adds `11.5%`/`11.9%` wall time at 48×36/96×72 for the dam break, and it moves up
to 45% of the particles across a 96×72 run, so a velocity-history-sensitive statistic can
change. Raw per-case JSON/CSV/NPZ, GIFs and `reseed_summary.{json,png}` are in
`outputs/m7/reseed/`.

## Follow-up — dam-break front diagnosis: the FLIP trend is temporal, not ill-posed

`python -m validation.dam_break_diagnosis` (about 5 minutes; `--fast` skips the 144x108 and
fine-`dt` cases). Seven front conventions were scored against the same 14 stations, and each
control was varied alone from a 95%-FLIP 96x72 baseline.

The 48 -> 96 rise is **not** a front-convention artefact: it survives rightmost,
rightmost `+h/2`, 99.5th percentile, both column rules and the two-cell-thickness rule, and it
is unchanged by the jitter seed (`<1.5%`) or nine particles per cell (`<3%`).

It is dominated by **time-step error**: at 96x72 the RMSE falls `.25645 -> .20607 -> .11451` for
`dt = 5e-4, 2.5e-4, 1.25e-4`, whereas the same first halving at 144x108 only moves
`.28490 -> .26382`. Measured delta at `tau=2.96` is 3.73 / 3.99 / 4.01 (48 / 96 / 144) against
3.67 in the experiment, and wall contact arrives at `tau = 2.90` on both 96 and 144 (2.44 on
48), so the front kinematics are grid-saturated while all grids overshoot. Refining removes
numerical damping that was coincidentally favourable on the coarse grid.

**Status: unmet as a convergence demonstration.** FLIP is grid-converged but its error limit is
set by missing physics (2-D, inviscid, no air, secondary digitisation), so refinement cannot
close the gap to the experiment. The earlier "does not converge" wording is superseded. Third
resolution and controls are in `outputs/m7/diagnosis/`.

## Follow-up — Strouhal resolution and blockage, separated

`python -m validation.cylinder_resolution` (studies `a`, `b`, `control`, plus `m6` to re-measure
the published cases); results in `outputs/m7/strouhal/`. Full tables and the per-pair
sensitivities are in `docs/milestones/M6.md`; the design record is D42.

The headline: **at D/H=.16 the reference gap is confinement and does not close** — refinement
raises St by `.0011 / .0049 / .0050` for 5.12 → 8 → 12 cells/D and halving `dt` raises it
`.0070`, all away from `.164335`. At **D/H=.08 the gap was resolution**: 5.12 → 8 cells/D moves

rung of the resolution ladder is **unmet** (about three hours at `2.4 s/step`) and is reported
as such rather than omitted. The legacy peak-to-peak estimator returns a doubled frequency on
the fast-wake cases; the cause (both extrema of each overshoot accepted as peaks, 32 samples per
cycle) is measured and documented rather than left as a discrepancy.

## Follow-up — the Ghia Re=400 outlier is a bad datum, not a bad solution

`python -m validation.ghia_outlier`; results in `outputs/m7/ghia/`, design record D43, detail in
`docs/milestones/M5.md`. The published Re=400 column has a secant-slope ratio of `17.96` at
`x=.9063` where the same measure gives `.70` and `.60` at Re=100 and Re=1000; a monotone cubic
through the other stations gives `-.365673`; and the grid ladder moves `-.325069 → -.361466`
from 48² to 128², towards that interpolation and away from the printed `-.23827`. The exclusion
from the primary metric is justified by the table's own inconsistency plus convergence.
